2. Event Overview & System Architecture

The Meraz Battleship Challenge is a 4-team simultaneous naval warfare competition. Unlike classic board games where you talk to one opponent, in Meraz, four physical ESP32 terminals compete concurrently across a wireless arena commanded by a dedicated Central Node referee.

Core Technology: ESP-NOW Protocol
ESP-NOW is a connectionless Wi-Fi packet technology developed by Espressif. It allows microcontrollers to communicate in under 5 milliseconds using raw radio packets, completely independent of local routers, internet gateways, or IP handshakes.

2.4 Status LEDs & Audio Buzzer Wiring (Choose Your Own Pins!)

Now that your display is wired and working, you need to add physical feedback devices: a Green Status LED, a Red Status LED, and an Audio Buzzer.

Important Rule: You Choose Your Own Output Pins!
We deliberately do not force you to use specific GPIO pins for the LEDs and Buzzer. Different ESP32 dev boards (30-pin vs 38-pin DevKit V1) label their pin headers differently. Your team will inspect your ESP32 board pinout, select free digital output pins, wire them up on the breadboard, and define them at the top of participant_starter.ino!

ESP32 Pin Selection Rules (What to Use and What to Avoid)

Pin Category GPIO Numbers Can You Use It for LEDs / Buzzer?
Safe General Output Pins Check free pins on your board (e.g. GPIO 25, 26, 27, 32, 33, 12, 13, 14, 21, 22) YES - Safe! Pick any free digital output pin from this group!
SPI Display Pins GPIO 2, 4, 5, 15, 18, 19, 23 NO - Do Not Use! These are already wired to your 2.4" TFT screen and touch controller.
Input-Only Pins GPIO 34, 35, 36 (VP), 39 (VN) NO - Do Not Use! These pins have no internal output transistors. They are strictly analog/digital inputs. You cannot drive an LED or Buzzer from them!
Boot Strapping & Flash Pins GPIO 0, 1 (TX0), 3 (RX0), 6-11 NO - Do Not Use! GPIO 0 is the bootloader trigger; GPIO 1/3 are USB Serial; GPIO 6-11 connect to internal flash memory.

Breadboard Electronics & Wiring Instructions

  • Green Status LED:
    • Purpose: Turns ON when it is YOUR TURN to fire! Turns OFF when your turn ends.
    • Wiring: Your chosen ESP32 GPIO → 220Ω current-limiting resistor → Long leg (Anode +) → Short leg (Cathode -) → Breadboard GND rail.
  • Red Status LED:
    • Purpose: Flashes rapidly when your attack shot HITS an opponent's ship!
    • Wiring: Your chosen ESP32 GPIO → 220Ω current-limiting resistor → Long leg (Anode +) → Short leg (Cathode -) → Breadboard GND rail.
  • Piezo Buzzer:
    • Purpose: Plays audio feedback for exactly two events:
      1. Alert sound when it is your turn to play.
      2. Hit confirmation beep when you hit an opponent's ship.
    • Wiring: Your chosen ESP32 GPIO → Buzzer positive pin (marked +); Buzzer negative pin → Breadboard GND rail.
Electronics 101: Why Current-Limiting Resistors are Mandatory!
LEDs have almost zero electrical resistance once they start conducting. If you connect an LED directly between 3.3V and GND without a resistor, an excessive current (>100mA) will surge through the semiconductor, instantly burning out the LED and permanently frying the ESP32 GPIO pin driver! A 220Ω to 330Ω resistor limits the current to a safe 8-12mA.

Pre-Built Firmware Architecture & Wire Subsystems

To help your team understand the underlying embedded engineering and protocol layers, below is an in-depth breakdown of how participant_starter.ino handles wireless communication and event rendering:

Subsystem 1 ESP-NOW Peer Registration (addCentralPeer() • Pre-Built)

1. Theoretical Concept & Networking Architecture:

In standard Wi-Fi, devices perform a three-way TCP/IP handshake, connect to an Access Point (router), and request dynamic IP addresses via DHCP. This process takes several seconds and introduces significant latency.

ESP-NOW bypasses all network layers and transmits raw Layer-2 802.11 Action Frames directly between radio transceivers in under 5 milliseconds. However, the ESP32 Wi-Fi baseband hardware features a hardware MAC filter: the radio will refuse to transmit unicast packets into the air until the destination MAC address has been registered in its internal Peer Table.

If code calls esp_now_send() without registering the peer first, the Wi-Fi driver aborts immediately with error code 12392 (ESP_ERR_ESPNOW_NOT_FOUND).

2. Data Structure Deep Dive: esp_now_peer_info_t:

Field Name Type Required Value Purpose & Explanation
peer_addr[6] uint8_t[6] centralMac The 6 raw octets of Central's physical Wi-Fi hardware MAC address.
channel uint8_t ESPNOW_CHANNEL (1) Wi-Fi 2.4GHz radio frequency channel. All tournament boards must use Channel 1.
encrypt bool false Encryption state. Must be false for lowest latency raw frame transmission.

3. Line-by-Line Code Walkthrough (Pre-Built in Sketch):

bool addCentralPeer() {
  // 1. Zero-initialize the struct. The '= {}' syntax clears all 32 bytes in RAM to 0x00,
  //    preventing garbage stack memory from corrupting internal interface flags.
  esp_now_peer_info_t peerInfo = {};

  // 2. Copy the 6 bytes of Central's MAC address into the peer_addr buffer.
  memcpy(peerInfo.peer_addr, centralMac, 6);

  // 3. Set the radio channel to Channel 1 (must match Central node).
  peerInfo.channel = 1;

  // 4. Disable encryption keys for maximum tournament throughput.
  peerInfo.encrypt = false;

  // 5. Register Central in the ESP32 hardware peer table.
  esp_err_t res = esp_now_add_peer(&peerInfo);

  if (res == ESP_OK) {
    Serial.println("[ESP-NOW] Central peer registered successfully!");
    return true;
  } else {
    Serial.printf("[ESP-NOW ERROR] Failed to add Central peer (err %d). Check centralMac!\n", res);
    return false;
  }
}
Subsystem 1 Diagnostics & Diagnostics:
  • Error 12392 (ESP_ERR_ESPNOW_NOT_FOUND): Means you tried sending a packet before calling addCentralPeer(), or Central's MAC was registered incorrectly.
  • Error 12391 (ESP_ERR_ESPNOW_EXIST): The peer was already registered. In ESP-NOW, calling esp_now_add_peer() twice for the same MAC without calling esp_now_del_peer() returns this warning.
  • Wrong MAC Address: Always check Central's boot Serial Monitor output (e.g. 6C:C8:40:86:FB:20) and verify that all 6 bytes match your centralMac[] array at the top of the file!

Subsystem 2 Fleet & Loadout Registration (sendRegistration() • Pre-Built)

1. Theoretical Concept & Tournament Handshake:

Central is the authoritative referee. No board can fire a shot until Central has verified its fleet. During the setup phase, your node must transmit its complete starting state: your assigned Team ID (1–4), your Team Name string, your 4 secret ship placements, and your locked 8-bit tactical loadout bitmask byte.

Central validates that your 4 ships do not overlap, fit strictly within the 7x7 grid, and total exactly 16 HP. If valid, Central adds your team to the active battle roster and replies with REG_OK.

2. Data Structure Deep Dive: RegistrationPacket (38 Bytes Total):

Offset Field Name Type & Size Purpose & In-Game Function
Byte 0 team_id uint8_t (1 byte) Your assigned team number (1, 2, 3, or 4).
Bytes 1-20 team_name char[20] (20 bytes) ASCII squad name displayed on Central's live projector dashboard.
Bytes 21-36 ships[4] ShipPlacement[4] (16 bytes) Your 4 warships encoded as (x, y, orientation, length) totaling 16 HP.
Byte 37 loadout uint8_t (1 byte) Packed bitmask of your 3 chosen power IDs.

3. Line-by-Line Code Walkthrough (Pre-Built in Sketch):

void sendRegistration() {
  RegistrationPacket pkt;

  // 1. Zero out all 38 bytes so uninitialized stack data does not leak over the radio
  memset(&pkt, 0, sizeof(pkt));

  // 2. Set our team ID (1, 2, 3, or 4)
  pkt.team_id = MY_TEAM_ID;

  // 3. Copy our team name safely (leaving room for the null-terminator)
  strncpy(pkt.team_name, MY_TEAM_NAME, sizeof(pkt.team_name) - 1);

  // 4. Copy all 4 secret ship placements (4 ships * 4 bytes = 16 bytes)
  memcpy(pkt.ships, myShips, sizeof(myShips));

  // 5. Pack our locked-in power loadout bitmask byte
  pkt.loadout = myLoadout;

  // 6. Transmit raw bytes to Central via ESP-NOW Layer-2 radio:
  esp_now_send(centralMac, (uint8_t *)&pkt, sizeof(pkt));

  Serial.print("Sent registration to Central (loadout 0x"); Serial.print(myLoadout, HEX); Serial.println(")...");
}

Subsystem 4 Tactical Powers Packaging (sendPower() • Pre-Built)

1. Theoretical Concept & Multi-Cell Targeting:

Unlike single-cell artillery, special powers have diverse targeting rules: Salvo fires 3 distinct artillery shells in a straight line; Double Strike fires 2 shells; Sonar sweeps a 3x3 box; Sea Mine arms an empty water cell; Repair fixes a damaged ship (non-sunk); Shield covers a 3x3 box on your fleet; and Smoke Screen deflects all attacks across your entire fleet for 1 round.

To support all 7 powers uniformly over the air with zero memory waste, all special weapons use a single unified 9-byte PowerUpPacket.

2. Data Structure Deep Dive: PowerUpPacket (9 Bytes Total):

Offset Field Name Type Value Range Description
Byte 0 power uint8_t 1 - 7 Power ID (1: Sonar, 2: Salvo, 3: Mine, 4: Repair, 5: Smoke, 6: Shield, 7: Double Strike).
Byte 1 team_id uint8_t 1 - 4 Your team number (MY_TEAM_ID).
Byte 2 target_id uint8_t 0 - 4 Target opponent ID (or 0 for defensive powers).
Bytes 3-4 x1, y1 uint8_t, uint8_t 0 - 6 First coordinate pair (used by Sonar center, Salvo #1, Double #1, Mine, Repair).
Bytes 5-6 x2, y2 uint8_t, uint8_t 0 - 6 Second coordinate pair (used by Salvo #2, Double #2).
Bytes 7-8 x3, y3 uint8_t, uint8_t 0 - 6 Third coordinate pair (used by Salvo #3).

3. Line-by-Line Code Walkthrough (Pre-Built in Sketch):

static bool sendPower(uint8_t power, uint8_t target, const uint8_t xs[3], const uint8_t ys[3]) {
  PowerUpPacket pkt;
  memset(&pkt, 0, sizeof(pkt));

  pkt.power     = power;
  pkt.team_id   = MY_TEAM_ID;
  pkt.target_id = target;
  pkt.x1 = xs[0]; pkt.y1 = ys[0];
  pkt.x2 = xs[1]; pkt.y2 = ys[1];
  pkt.x3 = xs[2]; pkt.y3 = ys[2];

  return esp_now_send(centralMac, (uint8_t *)&pkt, sizeof(pkt)) == ESP_OK;
}

Subsystem 5 Display Architecture & Real-Time Alerts (Pre-Built)

1. Theoretical Concept & Event-Driven GUI:

Microcontrollers cannot afford to redraw an entire LCD screen at 60 FPS in loop(). Redrawing the entire display takes 40–60ms over SPI, which causes severe screen flickering and starves the Wi-Fi stack of CPU time.

Instead, your terminal uses an event-driven GUI architecture. The display only redraws when triggered by incoming ESP-NOW radio packet events. In participant_starter.ino, incoming wireless game events are seamlessly connected to their corresponding UI renderers and hardware indicators:

2. Turn Synchronization (in handleTurnUpdate()):

Central regularly broadcasts a TurnUpdatePacket containing the active team (current_turn) and tournament state (round_state):

void handleTurnUpdate(TurnUpdatePacket *pkt) {
  currentTurnTeam = pkt->current_turn;
  roundState = pkt->round_state;

  if (roundState == STATE_RUNNING) {
    if (currentTurnTeam == MY_TEAM_ID) {
      Serial.println(">>> YOUR TURN <<<");

      // Switch screen to combat radar & enable firing buttons
      uiShowYourTurn();

      // Hardware Indicators: Solid Green LED + 1500 Hz chime
      setTurnLed(true);
      playTurnAlert();

    } else {
      Serial.print("Waiting - it is TEAM "); Serial.print(currentTurnTeam); Serial.println("'s turn.");

      // Show waiting screen indicating which enemy is currently aiming
      uiShowWaiting(currentTurnTeam);

      // Hardware Indicators: Extinguish Green LED
      setTurnLed(false);
    }
  } else if (roundState == STATE_GAMEOVER) {
    setTurnLed(false);
    uiShowMessage("GAME OVER");
  } else {
    setTurnLed(false);
    uiShowMessage("Waiting for start...");
  }
}

4. Packet Structures & Sizes (The Wire Protocol)

In ESP-NOW, communication is purely binary. We do not send strings like "ATTACK 2 3 4". Instead, we send raw C structs directly from RAM.

Why __attribute__((packed)) is Vital:

32-bit CPUs insert invisible 1-to-3-byte "padding gaps" between variables in RAM to keep data aligned with 32-bit CPU memory words. __attribute__((packed)) forces the compiler to pack every single byte tightly with zero padding gaps. This guarantees that your board and Central have an identical byte layout down to the individual bit!

Master Packet Summary Table:

Direction C Struct Name Exact Byte Size Transmission Frequency Purpose & Data Payload
TX (Send) RegistrationPacket 38 bytes Once on boot (retries every 3s) Registers your Team ID, Team Name, secret 4-ship fleet layout, and power loadout bitmask.
TX (Send) AttackPacket 4 bytes Once per turn when firing Fires a single strike shot: attacker team ID, target team ID, target X (0..6), target Y (0..6).
TX (Send) PowerUpPacket 9 bytes When activating special ability Triggers special power: power ID, team ID, target ID, up to 3 coordinate pairs (x1,y1, x2,y2, x3,y3).
RX (Recv) RegAckPacket 1 byte Immediately after registration Registration response: 0 = Accepted, 1 = Rejected, 2 = Reconnected.
RX (Recv) TurnUpdatePacket 2 bytes Every turn change (broadcast) Active team ID (1..4, or 0) and Match State (0=Setup, 1=Ready, 2=Running, 3=GameOver).
RX (Recv) FeedbackPacket 4 bytes Immediately after your attack Target team ID, shot coordinate (X, Y), and outcome code (Hit, Miss, Sunk, Mine, Shield, Smoke).
RX (Recv) PowerResultPacket 7 bytes After power activation Power ID, status (0=OK, 1=Rejected, 2=Jammed), target ID, Sonar count, shot results array.
Elegant Length-Based Routing:
Notice that every incoming packet from Central has a strictly unique byte size: RegAck (1 byte), TurnUpdate (2 bytes), Feedback (4 bytes), and PowerResult (7 bytes)! This means your receive callback doesn't need complex packet headers; it simply checks len to identify the packet type instantly!

Detailed Binary Struct Definitions

1. RegistrationPacket (38 Bytes)

typedef struct __attribute__((packed)) {
  uint8_t team_id;                 // 1 byte:  Assigned Team ID (1, 2, 3, or 4)
  char team_name[20];              // 20 bytes: Null-terminated name shown on projector
  ShipPlacement ships[NUM_SHIPS];  // 16 bytes: 4 ships x 4 bytes each
  uint8_t loadout;                 // 1 byte:  Bitmask of chosen powers
} RegistrationPacket;              // Total = 1 + 20 + 16 + 1 = 38 bytes

2. AttackPacket (4 Bytes)

typedef struct __attribute__((packed)) {
  uint8_t attacker_id;             // 1 byte: Your team ID (1..4)
  uint8_t target_id;               // 1 byte: Target opponent team ID (1..4)
  uint8_t x;                       // 1 byte: Target column (0..6)
  uint8_t y;                       // 1 byte: Target row (0..6)
} AttackPacket;                    // Total = 4 bytes

3. FeedbackPacket (4 Bytes)

typedef struct __attribute__((packed)) {
  uint8_t target_id;               // 1 byte: Target opponent team ID
  uint8_t x;                       // 1 byte: Target column
  uint8_t y;                       // 1 byte: Target row
  uint8_t result_code;             // 1 byte: Result code (0 to 7)
} FeedbackPacket;                  // Total = 4 bytes

// Feedback Result Codes:
//   0 = RESULT_MISS        (Hit open water, 0 pts)
//   1 = RESULT_HIT         (Struck an enemy ship, +10 pts!)
//   2 = RESULT_INVALID     (Illegal coordinate / out of turn)
//   3 = RESULT_SUNK        (Opponent entire fleet destroyed, +20 pts!)
//   4 = RESULT_MINE        (Struck a sea mine: miss + skip next turn)
//   5 = RESULT_BLOCKED     (Enemy Shield absorbed your shot)
//   6 = RESULT_UNKNOWN     (Enemy area obscured by Smoke Screen)
//   7 = RESULT_SHIP_SUNK   (One enemy ship destroyed, +15 pts!)

4. TurnUpdatePacket (2 Bytes)

typedef struct __attribute__((packed)) {
  uint8_t current_turn;            // 1 byte: Whose turn it is (1..4, or 0 if none)
  uint8_t round_state;             // 1 byte: 0=Setup, 1=Ready, 2=Running, 3=GameOver
} TurnUpdatePacket;                // Total = 2 bytes

5. The 7x7 Grid & 16 HP Fleet Layout Rules

The arena ocean is a 7x7 coordinate grid with columns X = 0..6 and rows Y = 0..6:

0 1 2 3 4 5 6 (X Column) +---+---+---+---+---+---+---+ 0 | . | . | . | . | . | . | . | 1 | . | . | . | . | . | . | . | 2 | . | . | . | . | . | . | . | 3 | . | . | . | . | . | . | . | (Y Row) 4 | . | . | . | . | . | . | . | 5 | . | . | . | . | . | . | . | 6 | . | . | . | . | . | . | . | +---+---+---+---+---+---+---+

Your 4-Ship Fleet (Exactly 16 HP Total)

Every team must deploy exactly 4 ships consisting of these lengths:

  • Ship 1 (5 cells): Straight: Horizontal, Vertical, or Diagonal (— / | / \)
  • Ship 2 (4 cells): Straight: Horizontal, Vertical, or Diagonal (— / | / \)
  • Ship 3 (4 cells): Straight: Horizontal, Vertical, or Diagonal (— / | / \)
  • Ship 4 (3 cells): Straight: Horizontal, Vertical, or Diagonal (— / | / \)
  • Total Fleet HP: 5 + 4 + 4 + 3 = 16 cells!

Ship Orientations & Special Shapes

Each ship is defined by { ship_len, start_x, start_y, orientation }:

Orientation Code Name Applicable Ships Coordinate Offset Geometry
0 ORIENT_HORIZONTAL Any ship (1, 2, 3, 4) Extends horizontally rightward: (x+i, y)
1 ORIENT_VERTICAL Any ship (1, 2, 3, 4) Extends vertically downward: (x, y+i)
2 ORIENT_DIAG_DOWN Any ship (1, 2, 3, 4) Extends diagonally down-right: (x+i, y+i)
3 ORIENT_DIAG_UP Any ship (1, 2, 3, 4) Extends diagonally up-right: (x+i, y-i)
The 3 Fatal Placement Mistakes:
  1. Out of Bounds: Any ship cell placed outside 0..6 causes immediate registration rejection.
  2. Overlapping Ships: If two of your ships share even a single cell, registration is rejected!
  3. Diagonal Edge Truncation: When using diagonal placement, remember that length 5 requires 5 full steps. E.g., starting at (3,0) diagonally down-right would end at (7,4), which is out of bounds!

Match Round States

  • State 0 (SETUP): Central is accepting registrations from all 4 participant boards.
  • State 1 (READY): All teams registered; match countdown initiated.
  • State 2 (RUNNING): Active combat! Central cycles turns (1 → 2 → 3 → 4 → 1). Each team has a turn countdown timer.
  • State 3 (GAME OVER): One team remains or time limit reached. Winner declared on screen.

10. Exhaustive Troubleshooting Guide ("If Nothing Works")

1. Display & Screen Issues

  • White Screen / Blank Screen: Check your wiring, especially VCC, GND, and LED (backlight power). Ensure User_Setup.h in the TFT_eSPI library folder has #define ILI9341_DRIVER uncommented and all pin numbers match the wiring table.
  • Colors Inverted (e.g. Blue looks Red): In setup(), add tft.invertDisplay(false); (or true) after tft.init();.
  • Touch Taps Not Responding: Verify the 4 touch lines (T_CLK, T_CS, T_DIN, T_DO). Ensure TOUCH_CS is defined as 15 in User_Setup.h.

2. Radio & ESP-NOW Issues

  • Central ignores my registration: Did you give the organizers your board's MAC address? Central's whitelist rejects unknown boards.
  • Send fails with error 12392 (ESP_ERR_ESPNOW_NOT_FOUND): You forgot to call esp_now_add_peer() before sending! Check that addCentralPeer() succeeded and centralMac matches Central's displayed MAC.
  • No turn updates received: Ensure ESPNOW_CHANNEL is set to 1. Wi-Fi radios cannot talk across different channels.

3. Breadboard LEDs & Buzzer Issues

  • LED does not light up: Check polarity (long leg = positive Anode, short leg = GND). Verify you didn't plug into an input-only pin (GPIO 34-39 cannot drive LEDs).
  • Buzzer makes no sound: Ensure your buzzer pin is configured as OUTPUT with pinMode(), and use the tone(pin, freq, duration) function. Note: Passive buzzers require tone(); active buzzers require digitalWrite(pin, HIGH).
  • ESP32 resets repeatedly (Brownout): Do NOT power the display backlight or buzzers from delicate 3.3V pins without adequate USB current. Use a good USB cable and port.

4. Game Logic & Rules Issues

  • Registration rejected (Code 1): One of your ships is out of bounds (>6) or two of your ships overlap each other. Check your coordinates in myShips[].
  • Attack rejected (RESULT_INVALID): You fired when it was not your turn, or fired at your own team ID, or targeted an invalid coordinate outside 0..6.
